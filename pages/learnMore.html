Understanding Aphasia

A comprehensive guide to understanding aphasia, its types, symptoms, and available support.

If you or someone you know is experiencing sudden difficulty speaking, seek immediate medical attention as it could be a sign of stroke.

What is Aphasia?

Aphasia is a language disorder that affects a person's ability to communicate. It typically occurs suddenly after a stroke or head injury, but can also develop gradually due to a brain tumor or progressive neurological disease.

Key Facts

  • Affects about 2 million Americans
  • Most common cause is stroke
  • Can impact speaking, listening, reading, and writing
  • Does not affect intelligence

Common Causes

  • Stroke
  • Head trauma
  • Brain tumors
  • Progressive neurological conditions

Types of Aphasia

Broca's Aphasia

Also known as expressive aphasia. Speech is difficult and limited to short phrases.

  • Slow, effortful speech
  • Difficulty forming complete sentences
  • Can understand speech well
  • Frustrated by communication difficulties

Wernicke's Aphasia

Also known as receptive aphasia. Speech is fluent but lacks meaning.

  • Fluent but meaningless speech
  • Poor comprehension
  • Unaware of speech errors
  • Use of made-up or incorrect words

Common Symptoms

  • Trouble finding words
  • Speaking in short or incomplete sentences
  • Speaking unrecognizable words
  • Substituting one word for another
